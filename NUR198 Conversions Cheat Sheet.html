Sanity check: kilograms are always the SMALLER number. A 200 lb client is about 90 kg. If your kg answer is bigger than the lb you started with, you multiplied when you should have divided.
Weight-based orders: convert the weight to kg FIRST, before you touch the dose. Doing it in the wrong order is the single most common weight-based math error.

🧠 How to set it up so you stop guessing

1. The formula for "how much do I give?"

(Desired ÷ Have) × Quantity = amount to give

Desired = what the order says. Have = the strength on the label. Quantity = the volume or tablets that strength comes in.

OrderLabel saysSet-upAnswer
metoprolol 50 mg25 mg tablets(50 ÷ 25) × 1 tab2 tablets
amoxicillin 500 mg250 mg per 5 mL(500 ÷ 250) × 5 mL10 mL
furosemide 30 mg40 mg per 4 mL(30 ÷ 40) × 4 mL3 mL

2. Convert BEFORE you divide

The formula only works when Desired and Have are in the same unit. If the order says grams and the bottle says milligrams, fix that first. This is where most wrong answers come from.

Order: 0.5 g. Label: 250 mg tablets.
Step 1 — same units: 0.5 g × 1000 = 500 mg
Step 2 — formula: (500 ÷ 250) × 1 = 2 tablets

3. Ask "does this answer make sense?"

Half a tablet, 2 tablets, 5–15 mL of a liquid: reasonable. 38 tablets or 0.006 mL: you slipped a decimal. On a test, an answer that is off by exactly 1000 almost always means a mg/mcg mix-up.

🚨 The traps they write questions about

Fluid ounce is NOT weight ounce. 1 fluid oz = 30 mL (volume). 1 weight oz = about 30 g (mass). Same word, different measurement. Read whether the question is about liquid the client drank or how much something weighs.
mg vs mcg is a 1000-fold error. This is the classic fatal medication error. Digoxin, levothyroxine and fentanyl are commonly ordered in mcg. Slow down whenever both units appear in one problem.
Leading zero YES, trailing zero NO. Write 0.5 mg, never .5 mg — the dot gets missed and it reads as 5 mg. Write 5 mg, never 5.0 mg — that reads as 50 mg. This is a test question, not just a style note.
Never abbreviate "units." Write out units. A "U" mistaken for a zero has caused 10-fold insulin overdoses. Same for "international units" — spell it out.
Household measuring is imprecise. Teach clients to use the dosing syringe or the cup that came with the medication, never a kitchen teaspoon. A kitchen "teaspoon" ranges from about 3 to 7 mL.
Rounding: follow whatever your course says. In general, tablets round to the nearest half or whole, mL for adults to the nearest tenth, and IV pump rates to the nearest whole mL/hr.

✏️ Practice — work it, then open it

Do it on paper first. The answer shows every step, not just the number.

1. Order: 0.75 g PO. Available: 250 mg tablets. How many tablets?
3 tablets
g -> mg is a SMALLER unit, so multiply by 1000 0.75 g x 1000 = 750 mg 750 mg / 250 mg per tab = 3 tablets
2. Order: 500 mcg. Available: 0.25 mg tablets. How many tablets?
2 tablets
Get both into the same unit first. 500 mcg / 1000 = 0.5 mg 0.5 mg / 0.25 mg per tab = 2 tablets
3. The provider orders 2 tablespoons of a liquid antacid. How many mL?
30 mL
1 tbsp = 15 mL 2 x 15 = 30 mL (also = 1 fluid ounce)
4. A client drank 1 cup of coffee, 6 oz of juice and 240 mL of water. Total intake in mL?
660 mL
1 cup = 240 mL 6 oz x 30 = 180 mL water = 240 mL 240 + 180 + 240 = 660 mL
5. A client weighs 176 lb. What is the weight in kg?
80 kg
1 kg = 2.2 lb, so DIVIDE by 2.2 176 / 2.2 = 80 kg
6. Order: 15 mg/kg for a client weighing 44 lb. How many mg?
300 mg
Convert the weight FIRST. 44 / 2.2 = 20 kg 20 kg x 15 mg/kg = 300 mg
7. Order: amoxicillin 375 mg PO. Available: 250 mg per 5 mL. How many mL? How many tsp?
7.5 mL = 1.5 tsp
Desired / Have x Quantity 375 / 250 x 5 = 7.5 mL 7.5 / 5 = 1.5 tsp
8. Convert 0.04 mg to mcg.
40 mcg
mg -> mcg is SMALLER, so multiply by 1000 0.04 x 1000 = 40 mcg (decimal hops 3 places RIGHT)
9. A client is to drink 1.5 L of fluid per day. How many 8 oz cups is that?
About 6 cups
1.5 L = 1500 mL 1 cup = 8 oz = 240 mL 1500 / 240 = 6.25, so about 6 cups
10. Infuse 1000 mL over 8 hours. What is the rate in mL/hr?
125 mL/hr
Total volume / total hours 1000 / 8 = 125 mL/hr
11. A child weighing 33 lb is ordered 5 mg/kg/day divided into 3 doses. How many mg per dose?
25 mg per dose
33 / 2.2 = 15 kg 15 x 5 = 75 mg per DAY 75 / 3 doses = 25 mg per dose
12. Available: 1 gram in 50 mL. The order is 400 mg. How many mL?
20 mL
1 g = 1000 mg 400 / 1000 x 50 = 20 mL
